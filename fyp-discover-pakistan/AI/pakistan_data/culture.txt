DISCOVER PAKISTAN — CULTURE & ETIQUETTE DATABASE
Complete Cultural Guide for Tourists Visiting Pakistan
================================================================

Pakistan is a country with an incredibly rich and diverse culture shaped by
thousands of years of civilization, multiple empires, Sufi Islam, and the
meeting of Central Asian, South Asian, and Middle Eastern traditions.
Understanding Pakistani culture will make your visit much more rewarding.

================================================================
SECTION 1: PEOPLE AND HOSPITALITY
================================================================

PAKISTANI HOSPITALITY — MEHMAN NAWAZI:
Pakistani hospitality is legendary and considered one of the best in the world.
The concept of "Mehman Nawazi" (guest hosting) is deeply embedded in culture.

What to Expect:
- Strangers will offer you tea, food, and help without expecting anything
- If invited to someone's home, you WILL be fed generously — do not refuse
- People will go out of their way to help tourists find directions
- Shopkeepers will offer tea while you browse — this is normal and friendly
- Children will want photos with foreign tourists — politely accept or decline

How to Respond:
- Say "Shukriya" (thank you) genuinely
- Accept tea when offered (refusing can seem rude)
- Show interest in local culture — Pakistanis love sharing their heritage
- Reciprocate kindness — small gestures go a long way

ETHNIC GROUPS AND REGIONS:
Pakistan has four main provinces, each with distinct culture:

1. PUNJAB (Lahore, Islamabad, Faisalabad):
   - Punjabi culture: Vibrant, festive, loud, warm
   - Famous for: Food, music (bhangra), festivals, agriculture
   - Language: Punjabi and Urdu
   - Personality: Very welcoming, love to host and feed guests

2. SINDH (Karachi, Hyderabad):
   - Sindhi culture: Ancient, mystical, Sufi-influenced
   - Famous for: Ajrak (traditional cloth), Sindhi cap, ancient civilization
   - Language: Sindhi and Urdu
   - Personality: Proud of ancient heritage, hospitable

3. KHYBER PAKHTUNKHWA — KPK (Peshawar):
   - Pashtun culture: Known for Pashtunwali code of honor
   - Pashtunwali: Code requiring hospitality, protection of guests, justice
   - Famous for: Chapli kebab, bravery, tribal traditions
   - Language: Pashto and Urdu
   - Personality: Serious, extremely hospitable once trust established

4. BALOCHISTAN (Quetta):
   - Baloch culture: Nomadic heritage, tribal structure
   - Famous for: Sajji, music, handmade carpets
   - Language: Balochi, Brahui, Pashto
   - Personality: Reserved with strangers, extremely generous with guests

SECTION 2: RELIGION AND RELIGIOUS CUSTOMS
================================================================

ISLAM IN PAKISTAN:
Pakistan is an Islamic Republic — 97% Muslim population.
Islam deeply influences daily life, culture, food, festivals, and customs.

DAILY PRAYER TIMES (NAMAZ):
Five prayers daily — tourists should be aware:
- Fajr: Before sunrise (shops may be closed)
- Zuhr: Midday (Friday most important — shops close 12pm-2pm)
- Asr: Afternoon
- Maghrib: Sunset (beautiful call to prayer, everything slows down)
- Isha: Night

MOSQUE ETIQUETTE (For Tourists):
- Remove shoes before entering — always, no exceptions
- Dress modestly: cover shoulders and knees (both men and women)
- Women: Bring a scarf to cover hair inside mosque
- Enter quietly and respectfully
- Do not walk in front of someone who is praying
- Photography: Usually allowed except during prayer time (ask first)
- Non-Muslims are welcome at most mosques except prayer hall during prayer
- Speak softly inside mosque premises

FRIDAY — JUMU'AH:
Most important day of the week for Muslims.
- Friday midday prayer (12pm-2pm) is obligatory for Muslim men
- Many shops and offices close during this time
- Plan your Friday accordingly — avoid scheduling activities 12pm-2pm
- Government offices may be closed or have reduced hours

RAMADAN (DATES CHANGE EACH YEAR):
Ramadan is the Islamic holy month of fasting.
Muslims fast from dawn to sunset (no food, no water, no smoking).

For Tourists During Ramadan:
- DO NOT eat, drink, or smoke in public during fasting hours — it is
  disrespectful and may be illegal in some areas
- Many restaurants are closed during the day, open at Iftar (sunset)
- Iftar (breaking fast at sunset) is a beautiful community experience
  — you may be invited to join, accept if possible
- Sehri (pre-dawn meal) — streets come alive at 3-4am
- Night atmosphere during Ramadan is special and festive
- Ramadan is actually a great time to experience Pakistani culture

EID AL-FITR AND EID AL-ADHA:
Two major Islamic festivals.
- Eid Al-Fitr: End of Ramadan, 3 days of celebration
- Eid Al-Adha: 3 months after Ramadan, sacrifice of animals
- During Eid: Most shops, offices closed for 2-4 days
- Cities are festive, families visit each other, special foods
- Tourists may receive Eid gifts and invitations — a special experience
- Book hotels and transport in advance — very busy period

SECTION 3: DRESS CODE AND APPEARANCE
================================================================

GENERAL DRESS CODE:
Pakistan is a conservative Muslim country. Modest dressing is important
for showing respect and avoiding unwanted attention.

FOR WOMEN TOURISTS:
- Cover shoulders and arms (no sleeveless tops in public)
- Cover legs — no shorts in public (below knee preferred)
- Loose-fitting clothes preferred over tight clothes
- Carry a dupatta (scarf) — useful for mosques and rural areas
- At religious sites: Full head covering required
- In cities like Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad: Slightly more relaxed
- In rural areas and small towns: Be more conservative
- Salwar kameez (Pakistani traditional dress) is ideal — comfortable and respectful
  Buy one at local markets for PKR 500-2,000

FOR MEN TOURISTS:
- Shorts are acceptable in tourist areas and hotels but avoid in rural areas
- Avoid sleeveless shirts in conservative areas
- At mosques: Cover knees and shoulders
- Shalwar kameez (traditional dress) is comfortable and appreciated by locals

COLORS AND DRESS STYLE:
- Pakistani clothing is colorful and embroidered — embrace it!
- Traditional fabrics: Cotton (summer), khaddar (winter), lawn (summer)
- Buying local clothes is highly recommended — comfortable and respectful

SECTION 4: SOCIAL CUSTOMS AND ETIQUETTE
================================================================

GREETINGS:
- Islamic greeting: "Assalam-o-Alaikum" (Peace be upon you)
  Response: "Wa-Alaikum-Assalam"
- Use this greeting — Pakistanis will be delighted a tourist knows it
- Formal: Right hand on chest with slight bow
- Between friends: Handshakes (men to men, women to women)
- Important: Avoid shaking hands with opposite gender unless they initiate
  (Some conservative Pakistanis do not shake hands with opposite gender)

PHYSICAL CONTACT NORMS:
- Men greeting men: Handshake, sometimes hug
- Women greeting women: Handshake or cheek kiss (among friends)
- Men and women: Generally avoid physical contact in public
- Holding hands in public: Only between same gender (common friendship gesture)
- PDA (public display of affection) between couples: Not acceptable

TAKING PHOTOGRAPHS:
- Always ask permission before photographing people
- Most Pakistanis are happy to be photographed (and will ask for selfies!)
- At religious sites: Ask if photography is allowed
- Military installations, airports, sensitive areas: NEVER photograph
- Women: Some may decline to be photographed — always respect this

BARGAINING IN BAZAARS:
- Bargaining is expected and normal — do not feel awkward
- Start at 40-50% of asking price
- Seller will come down, you go up, meet somewhere in middle
- Be friendly during bargaining — it's a social interaction, not a fight
- Walk away if price is too high — seller may call you back with lower price
- Fixed price shops (supermarkets, chain stores): No bargaining needed
- Souvenirs, handicrafts, clothes bazaars: Always bargain

HOME VISITS:
If invited to a Pakistani home (which may happen spontaneously):
- Remove shoes at the door — always
- Bring a small gift if possible (sweets are perfect)
- Accept food offered — refusing can offend
- Compliment the food and home
- Eat with right hand
- Sit cross-legged on floor if that's the setting — it's normal
- Women may dine separately from men in more conservative homes
- Tea is always served — drink it

SECTION 5: FESTIVALS AND CELEBRATIONS
================================================================

BASANT (SPRING FESTIVAL) — Punjab:
Traditional kite-flying festival celebrating spring.
Banned in some years due to accidents but culturally significant.
When held: February, Lahore famous for it.
Yellow and spring colors worn, rooftop parties, music.

SHANDUR POLO FESTIVAL:
Location: Shandur Pass, highest polo ground in world (3,700m)
When: July every year
Teams: Gilgit vs Chitral — traditional rivals
One of the most unique sporting events in the world.
Camping at 3,700m altitude with folk music and culture.

CHOLISTAN JEEP RALLY:
Location: Cholistan Desert, Bahawalpur, Punjab
When: February
Jeep racing through the Cholistan Desert.
Colorful event with folk music and traditional culture.

KALASH FESTIVALS (KPK):
The Kalash people of Chitral (non-Muslim minority) celebrate unique festivals:
- Chilam Joshi (May): Spring festival with dancing and music
- Uchal (August): Thanksgiving festival
- Choimus (December): Winter solstice festival
Colorful traditional costumes, unique non-Islamic culture in Pakistan.

EID CELEBRATIONS:
Both Eids are national holidays.
Streets decorated, markets busy, families celebrate.
Special foods: Sheer khurma, sewaiyyan (vermicelli dessert), biryani.
Unique experience for tourists — you may be invited to join celebrations.

INDEPENDENCE DAY — 14 AUGUST:
Pakistan's Independence Day — one of the most festive days.
Green and white everywhere (Pakistan's colors).
Large gatherings at Minar-e-Pakistan Lahore, national monuments.
Fireworks, cultural performances, flag-raising ceremonies.

LABOUR DAY — 1 MAY:
National holiday, most businesses closed.

SECTION 6: ARTS, CRAFTS, AND SHOPPING
================================================================

TRADITIONAL CRAFTS TO BUY:
1. Multani Blue Pottery — hand-painted, unique to Multan
2. Kashmiri Shawls — embroidered woolen shawls
3. Sindhi Ajrak — block-printed cloth, traditional Sindhi
4. Camel Leather Goods — Karachi and Rawalpindi
5. Onyx Stone Items — Balochistan, cheap and unique
6. Truck Art Items — colorful Pakistani truck art on everyday items
7. Wooden Handicrafts from Swat
8. Peshawari Chappal (sandals) — very durable, famous

TRUCK ART:
One of Pakistan's most unique cultural exports.
Pakistani trucks are decorated with elaborate paintings, poetry, and designs.
Has become internationally famous — look for truck art inspired souvenirs.

MUSIC OF PAKISTAN:
- Classical: Qawwali (Sufi devotional music), Ghazal
- Famous Qawwali: Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (world famous singer from Pakistan)
- Folk: Bhangra (Punjab), Attan (KPK/Pashtun), Jhumar (Balochistan)
- Modern: Pop, Rock, Coke Studio (internationally acclaimed music show)
- Live music: Data Darbar shrine (Lahore) has qawwali every Thursday night

SUFI SHRINES AND MUSIC:
Every Thursday night at Data Darbar (Lahore) and many shrines:
Free qawwali performances continue until late night.
Anyone can attend — unique spiritual and musical experience.
Dress modestly, participate respectfully.

SECTION 7: LANGUAGE GUIDE FOR TOURISTS
================================================================

LANGUAGES OF PAKISTAN:
- Urdu: National language, spoken/understood nationwide
- English: Official language, spoken in cities and business settings
- Punjabi: Most widely spoken native language (Punjab)
- Sindhi: Sindh province
- Pashto: KPK and Balochistan
- Balochi: Balochistan
- Brahui: Balochistan
- Shina/Balti/Burushaski: Northern areas

USEFUL URDU FOR TOURISTS:
(Pronunciation guides in brackets)

BASIC:
- Hello: Assalam-o-Alaikum [as-SA-lam-o-a-LAY-kum]
- Goodbye: Khuda Hafiz [khu-DA ha-FIZ]
- Yes: Ji Haan / Haan [jee haan]
- No: Nahi / Ji Nahi [na-HEE]
- Please: Meherbani se / Kripya [meh-er-BA-nee]
- Thank you: Shukriya / Meherbani [shuk-REE-ya]
- Sorry: Maafi chahta/chahti hoon [MA-fi]
- Excuse me: Maafi / Suniye [su-NEE-yay]

QUESTIONS:
- How much?: Kitna hai? [KIT-na hai]
- Where is...?: ... kahan hai? [ka-HAN hai]
- What is this?: Yeh kya hai? [yeh kya hai]
- Do you speak English?: Aap angrezi bolte hain? 
- I don't understand: Mujhe samajh nahi aya
- Can you help me?: Aap meri madad kar sakte hain?

DIRECTIONS:
- Right: Dahini taraf [da-HEE-ni]
- Left: Bayi taraf [BA-yee]
- Straight: Seedha [SEED-ha]
- Near: Qareeb [ka-REEB]
- Far: Dur [door]
- Stop here: Yahan rokein [ya-HAN]

FOOD AND RESTAURANTS:
- I am hungry: Mujhe bhook lagi hai
- Water: Paani [PA-ni]
- Food: Khana [KA-na]
- Bill: Hisaab / Bill [his-AB]
- Delicious: Mazedaar / Bahut acha [ma-ze-DAR]
- More please: Aur dijiye [OR dee-jee-yay]
- Without spice: Mirchi kam / Bina mirchi
- Very spicy: Bohot tez [bo-HOT tez]
- Sweet: Meetha [MEE-tha]

NUMBERS:
- 1: Ek | 2: Do | 3: Teen | 4: Chaar | 5: Paanch
- 6: Chhe | 7: Saat | 8: Aath | 9: Nau | 10: Das
- 100: Sau | 1000: Ek Hazaar

EMERGENCIES:
- Help!: Madad karo! / Bachao!
- Police: Police [po-LEES]

- I am lost: Main kho gaya/gayi hoon
