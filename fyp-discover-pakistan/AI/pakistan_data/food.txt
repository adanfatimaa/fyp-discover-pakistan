DISCOVER PAKISTAN — FOOD & CUISINE DATABASE
Complete Guide to Pakistani Food, Restaurants & Street Food
================================================================

Pakistani cuisine is one of the richest and most diverse in the world.
Each region has its own unique dishes, cooking styles, and ingredients.
This guide covers everything a tourist needs to know about eating in Pakistan.

================================================================
SECTION 1: ICONIC PAKISTANI DISHES (MUST TRY)
================================================================

--- BIRYANI ---
Type: Rice dish
Region: Nationwide, originated in Sindh/Karachi style most famous
Description: Aromatic basmati rice cooked with spiced meat (chicken/beef/mutton),
saffron, and whole spices. Considered Pakistan's national dish by many.

Varieties:
- Karachi Biryani: Spicy, red color, potatoes added, most famous version
- Sindhi Biryani: With fried onions and green chilies
- Lahori Biryani: Milder, more ghee-based
- Memoni Biryani: Very spicy, from Memon community Karachi
- Afghani Biryani (Pulao style): Less spicy, more fragrant

Where to Eat Best Biryani:
- Karachi: Student Biryani (original Burns Road branch, 50+ years old)
- Karachi: Waheed Biryani (Malir area, very popular)
- Lahore: Delhi Darbar Restaurant
- Islamabad: Savour Foods Biryani Counter

Price: PKR 250-500 per plate

--- KARAHI ---
Type: Main course, meat dish
Named After: The wok-shaped cooking vessel (karahi/kadhai)
Description: Meat (chicken, mutton, or beef) cooked in tomatoes, ginger,
garlic, green chilies and spices in a heavy iron wok over high flame.

Varieties:
- Lahori Karahi: With tomatoes, ginger, green chilies — most common
- Peshawari Karahi (White Karahi): NO tomatoes, cooked in fat/butter/cream
  — unique, creamy, best version in Pakistan according to many
- Kohat Karahi: With dried tomatoes
- Namak Mandi Karahi: Peshawar style, cooked in pure ghee (clarified butter)

Where to Eat Best Karahi:
- Peshawar: Charsi Tikka, Namak Mandi (MUST TRY, been there for decades)
- Peshawar: Peshawari Inn, Namak Mandi
- Lahore: Butt Karahi, Burns Road
- Islamabad: Savour Foods, G-9

Price: PKR 800-2,500 for half/full karahi (serves 2-4 people)

--- NIHARI ---
Type: Breakfast/Main course
Origin: Mughal era, Old Delhi — brought to Pakistan in 1947
Description: Slow-cooked beef/mutton stew with bone marrow, cooked overnight
with wheat flour and special spice mix (nihari masala). Rich, thick curry.
Traditionally eaten for breakfast with naan/kulcha bread.

Best Nihari in Pakistan:
- Lahore: Waris Nihari, Lakshmi Chowk (open since 1950s, very famous)
- Lahore: Phajja Nihari, Burns Road (60+ year old establishment)
- Karachi: Zahid Nihari, Burns Road
- Islamabad: Delhi Nihari, various locations

Price: PKR 400-800 per serving

--- CHAPLI KEBAB ---
Type: Appetizer / Main
Origin: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Pashtun specialty)
Description: Flat, wide, disc-shaped kebab made from minced beef mixed with
onions, tomatoes, coriander seeds, green chilies, and eggs. Shallow fried.
Crispy outside, juicy inside. Arguably Pakistan's most flavorful kebab.

Where to Eat:
- Peshawar: Charsi Tikka, Namak Mandi (LEGENDARY)
- Peshawar: Any restaurant in Qissa Khwani Bazaar
- Everywhere in KPK: Local dhabbas (roadside restaurants)
- Lahore/Islamabad: Charsi Tikka chain restaurants

Price: PKR 200-400 per piece (large size, one piece can be a meal)

--- SAJJI ---
Type: Main course, celebration dish
Origin: Balochistan (Quetta)
Description: Whole lamb or chicken marinated in salt and minimal spices,
skewered on a long metal rod, slow-roasted over open fire for hours.
The meat falls off the bone and has a unique smoky flavor.
Considered the pride of Baloch cuisine.

Best Sajji:
- Quetta: Lal's Restaurant (most famous in Pakistan)
- Quetta: Any traditional Sajji restaurant near Qissa Khwani or city center
- Karachi: Several Baloch restaurants in Lyari and Keamari areas

Price: PKR 1,500-4,000 for whole chicken/half lamb

--- HALEEM ---
Type: Main course / Street food
Description: Slow-cooked mixture of lentils, wheat, barley, and meat (beef or mutton)
cooked for 7-8 hours until everything blends into a thick porridge-like consistency.
Topped with fried onions, ginger julienne, lemon, and green chilies.
Very nutritious, high protein dish.

Best Haleem:
- Karachi: Sabri Nihari (also famous for Haleem)
- Lahore: Bundu Khan Restaurant
- Islamabad: Many local restaurants

Hyderabadi Haleem: Most famous variety, very popular in Karachi
Price: PKR 300-600 per serving

--- PAYE (TROTTERS) ---
Type: Breakfast specialty
Description: Goat or cow trotters (feet) slow-cooked overnight in spices
until the gelatin dissolves and creates a thick, rich broth.
Traditionally eaten for breakfast, believed to give strength.

Best Paye:
- Lahore: Phajja Siri Paye, Burns Road (ICONIC, since 1947)
- Lahore: Syed Paye, Lakshmi Chowk
Price: PKR 300-600 per serving

SECTION 2: STREET FOOD (CHEAPEST AND MOST DELICIOUS)
================================================================

--- GOLGAPPAY / PANI PURI ---
Description: Hollow crispy shells filled with spiced water (imli/tamarind),
chickpeas, potatoes. Eaten in one bite. Very popular street snack.
Price: PKR 50-150 for 6-8 pieces
Where: Every city, street vendors everywhere

--- BUN KEBAB ---
City: Karachi specialty
Description: Spiced beef/lentil patty in a bun with chutney and egg.
Pakistan's version of a burger, infinitely more flavorful.
Price: PKR 80-200
Best: Burns Road, Stadium Road, Karachi

--- SAMOSA ---
Description: Triangular pastry stuffed with spiced potatoes/meat, deep fried.
Available nationwide, eaten as snack or with tea.
Price: PKR 30-80 per piece

--- PAKORA ---
Description: Vegetables or meat dipped in gram flour batter, deep fried.
Classic monsoon season and Ramadan snack.
Varieties: Onion, potato, spinach, chicken pakora
Price: PKR 20-50 each

--- DAHI BHALLE ---
City: Lahore specialty, also popular nationwide
Description: Lentil dumplings soaked in yogurt, topped with tamarind chutney,
mint chutney, and spices. Cold, refreshing street snack.
Price: PKR 100-250

--- CHAAT ---
Description: Tangy, spicy mixture of chickpeas, potatoes, chutneys, yogurt.
Many varieties: fruit chaat, dahi chaat, aloo chaat.
Price: PKR 100-300

--- TIKKA / SEEKH KEBAB ---
Description: Marinated chicken pieces (tikka) or minced meat on skewers (seekh)
grilled over charcoal. Found at BBQ stalls everywhere.
Price: PKR 200-400 per portion
Where: Burns Road Karachi, Lahore food streets, Namak Mandi Peshawar

SECTION 3: BREAKFAST FOODS
================================================================

--- HALWA PURI ---
Description: Traditional Pakistani breakfast — puri (fried bread) served with
semolina halwa (sweet), chickpea curry (chanay), and aloo bhujia (potato curry).
Classic Sunday breakfast in Punjab.
Price: PKR 200-400 per set
Best in Lahore: Siddique Halwa Puri, Lakshmi Chowk

--- PARATHA ---
Description: Layered flatbread made with whole wheat flour and ghee/oil,
cooked on a flat griddle. Can be plain, stuffed with potato (aloo paratha),
egg (anda paratha), or minced meat.
Served with yogurt, pickle, or lassi.
Price: PKR 50-150 each

--- QEHWA ---
Type: Traditional tea
Region: KPK, Gilgit-Baltistan, Pashtun culture
Description: Green tea brewed with cardamom, saffron, cinnamon, cloves.
Sometimes with nuts (almonds, pine nuts). Served without milk.
Very refreshing, especially after a heavy meal.
Price: PKR 100-300 per cup/pot

--- CHAI (TEA) ---
Pakistan's most consumed beverage.
Doodh Pati: Milk tea brewed with tea leaves directly in milk.
Very strong, sweet, served in small cups (kulhad/earthen cups at dhabas).
Price: PKR 30-100

SECTION 4: REGIONAL SPECIALTIES BY CITY
================================================================

LAHORE MUST EAT:
1. Nihari at Waris Nihari (Lakshmi Chowk) — 70 year old institution
2. Paye at Phajja Siri Paye (Burns Road) — since 1947
3. Halwa Puri at any Lakshmi Chowk restaurant (breakfast)
4. Lahori Chargha — whole fried chicken, very crispy
5. Dahi Bhalle from street vendors near Anarkali
6. Kulfi Faluda from Anarkali Bazaar
7. Fort Road Food Street (evening) — outdoor dining, view of Badshahi Mosque

KARACHI MUST EAT:
1. Biryani at Student Biryani, Burns Road (original branch)
2. Bun Kebab from Stadium Road or Burns Road vendors
3. Haleem at Sabri Nihari
4. Seekh Kebab at BBQ Tonight, Burns Road
5. Nihari at Zahid Nihari
6. Do Darya waterfront restaurants for seafood
7. Boat Basin area for variety

PESHAWAR MUST EAT:
1. Chapli Kebab at Charsi Tikka, Namak Mandi
2. Peshawari Karahi (white karahi) at any Namak Mandi restaurant
3. Kabuli Pulao (Afghan-style rice) — very fragrant
4. Qehwa tea at traditional tea houses
5. Fresh dry fruits from Qissa Khwani Bazaar

ISLAMABAD MUST EAT:
1. Monal Restaurant (Margalla Hills) — view + food + ambiance
2. Savour Foods — famous Pakistani BBQ
3. F-7 and F-6 areas for cafe culture and modern restaurants
4. Saidpur Village restaurants for traditional atmosphere

HUNZA / NORTH MUST EAT:
1. Chapshuro — Hunzakut meat pastry, unique to region
2. Mamtu — Hunza-style dumplings
3. Dried apricots — buy fresh from farms
4. Hunza water — drink from natural springs
5. Organic walnut and mulberry products

QUETTA MUST EAT:
1. Sajji — Lal's Restaurant (best in Pakistan)
2. Kaak bread with tea
3. Dried fruits (cheapest prices here)

SECTION 5: DESSERTS AND SWEETS
================================================================

--- KHEER ---
Description: Rice pudding cooked in full-fat milk with sugar, cardamom,
saffron, and rose water. Topped with almonds and pistachios.
Pakistan's most common dessert.

--- GULAB JAMUN ---
Description: Soft milk-solid dumplings deep fried and soaked in sugar syrup.
Served hot or at room temperature. Very sweet.
Price: PKR 50-150 for 2-3 pieces

--- KULFI ---
Description: Pakistani ice cream — denser, creamier, more milky than regular.
Flavors: Mango, pistachio, rose, malai (cream).
Famous: Kulfi Faluda (with vermicelli noodles and rose syrup)
Best in Lahore: Anarkali Bazaar vendors

--- SOHAN HALWA ---
City: Multan specialty (sold everywhere but Multan is best)
Description: Hard, brittle sweet made from wheat germ, ghee, sugar, and nuts.
Famous gift to take from Multan.
Price: PKR 800-1,500 per kg

--- JALEBI ---
Description: Spiral-shaped fried batter soaked in sugar syrup. Orange colored.
Eaten hot, crispy. Often paired with Halwa Puri for breakfast.
Price: PKR 30-80 each

--- BARFI ---
Description: Dense milk-based fudge in various flavors.
Varieties: Plain, pistachio, coconut, mango.
Famous: Kaju (cashew) barfi, Doda barfi (Peshawar specialty)

SECTION 6: DRINKS
================================================================

--- LASSI ---
Description: Traditional yogurt-based drink, can be sweet or salty.
Sweet Lassi: With sugar, sometimes mango or rose flavor
Salty Lassi: With salt and sometimes cumin
Best in Lahore: Multiple lassi shops near Anarkali and Walled City
Price: PKR 100-250

--- SUGARCANE JUICE (GANNAY KA RUS) ---
Very popular street drink, pressed fresh.
Often mixed with lemon and ginger.
Price: PKR 50-150 per glass

--- ROOH AFZA SHARBAT ---
Rose-flavored drink, iconic Pakistani summer beverage.
Mixed with milk or water. Very refreshing.
Available at all restaurants and homes.

--- FRESH JUICES ---
Widely available: Mango (summer), Orange, Apple, Pomegranate
Price: PKR 100-300

SECTION 7: DINING ETIQUETTE AND TIPS FOR TOURISTS
================================================================

EATING CUSTOMS:
- Eating with right hand is traditional (using left hand is considered rude)
- Guests are always offered food first — accept graciously
- Leaving food on plate is okay (means you're satisfied)
- You may be asked to share a table at busy restaurants — normal and friendly

RESTAURANT TYPES:
1. Dhaba: Roadside simple restaurant, cheapest, most authentic
2. Tandoor: Bread bakery shop, buy fresh naan/roti
3. Restaurant: Formal sit-down, menu, slightly expensive
4. Hotel Restaurant: Most expensive, international food also available
5. Food Street: Outdoor dining areas, good for tourists

HALAL FOOD:
All food in Pakistan is Halal (no pork anywhere).
Beef, mutton, chicken, fish are all available.
Vegetarian options available but Pakistan is primarily a meat-eating culture.

HYGIENE TIPS:
- Ask if water is filtered/bottled before drinking
- Well-established restaurants are generally safe
- Street food at busy stalls (high turnover) is usually fresh and safe
- Avoid cut fruits displayed openly in heat

TIPPING:
- Not mandatory but appreciated
- 5-10% of bill at restaurants
- PKR 50-200 for small services
- Some places add 5-10% service charge automatically

POPULAR FOOD APPS IN PAKISTAN:
- foodpanda (most popular delivery app)
- Cheetay
- Most restaurants in cities also have WhatsApp ordering
