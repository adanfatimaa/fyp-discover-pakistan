Visiting Pakistan for the First Time?

Everything you need to know before your first trip to Pakistan

Travel Basics

Visa Policy

Pakistan offers e-visa facilities for many countries. Submit your application 7–10 days in advance. You can apply here e-Visa Application

Connectivity

Get a SIM at the airport (Jazz or Zong) for best 4G coverage.

Currency

The official currency is the Pakistani Rupee. Carry some cash in for local bazaars and small shops, as card payments may not always be available in smaller markets. Cards work in major malls.

Cultural Norms

Dress Code

Dress modestly in public places, especially when visiting religious or rural areas.

Respect

Respect local customs and traditions; polite behavior is highly valued.

Shoes Off

Remove shoes before entering mosques and some homes as a sign of respect.

Safety First

Transport

Get around cities safely using apps like InDrive. For a fun local experience, try a rickshaw, just hold on tight, it’s like a rollercoaster on wheels!

Scams

Be cautious of overcharging, fake guides, or too-good-to-be-true deals in tourist areas.

Health

Drink bottled or filtered water and avoid street food if your stomach is sensitive. Carry basic medicines such as painkillers, motion sickness tablets.

Local Etiquette

Greetings

Greet with a polite “Assalam o alaikum”. A handshake is common between men. For opposite genders, a hand over the heart with a nod is polite.

Public Behavior

Public displays of affection are culturally inappropriate. Maintain courteous distance.

Food Etiquette

Accept tea or snacks when offered, it’s polite. Use your right hand for eating and serving.

Mistakes to Avoid

Overpacking

Clothes are cheap and easy to buy locally. Save space for souvenirs.

Photography

Ask before taking photos of locals, especially women, or military sites.

Weather Ignorance

North is cold, South is hot. Check forecasts for specific cities.

Timing

Everything shuts down on Friday afternoons for prayers. Plan accordingly.

Language Basics

Greetings

Say “Assalam o alaikum” to greet politely, locals appreciate it.

Polite Words

Use “Shuk-ri-ya” for saying thank you.

Complimenting

“Bo-hat ach-a” means “Very good” or “Well done.” A nice way to compliment someone.
